What it does

Two directions, one small app

Most setups keep Home Assistant in a browser tab. HA DeskLink moves the handful of things you actually check all day onto the desktop — and feeds your computer's own status back into your automations.

Live sensors on the desktop

Temperatures, energy, locks, motion — values from your instance, visible at a glance in the tray.

Your PC becomes an entity

CPU load, memory, disk and uptime are pushed into Home Assistant as regular sensors.

Controls from the tray

Toggle lights, switches and scenes from the tray menu without opening a browser window.

Notifications that arrive

Automations and events surface as native desktop notifications, with your choice of position and monitor.

Full dashboard when you want it

An optional embedded WebView gives you the complete Lovelace UI inside the app.

Set it up once

Auto-update, autostart, dark and light theme, hotkeys. Then forget it's there.

Context

Where it fits

Browser tab or PWA. The full dashboard, but it competes with every other tab you have open.

HASS.Agent. A capable Windows-only integration with sensors and commands.

HA DeskLink. A lightweight companion for Windows, Linux and macOS: live desktop view, notifications, PC sensors and an embedded dashboard — without running a browser.

Get involved

Testers shape the roadmap

HA DeskLink improves fastest with feedback from real users. Two platforms especially need eyes on them right now:

Linux. Actively developed, but barely tested outside my own setups — edge cases are almost guaranteed to exist. If you run it, tell me what breaks.

macOS. Development is paused simply because there is no Mac to test on and too few users yet. If enough people show interest and help test, development picks up again — that decision is directly in your hands.

Every bug report, edge case or "works on my setup" helps. Testers and feedback are explicitly welcome — whether you find a crash, a visual glitch, or just want to suggest a feature.
